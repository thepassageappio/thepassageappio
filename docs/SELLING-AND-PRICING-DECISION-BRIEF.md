# Passage Authority selling and pricing decision brief

## The category in one sentence

Passage Authority is delegated-authority operations software for financial institutions, starting with the end-to-end intake, review, decision, and lifecycle of financial powers of attorney.

## Four-sentence pitch

Power of attorney requests often move through branches, inboxes, document folders, and review queues without one clear current record. Passage Authority gives the institution one guided workflow to collect the right inputs, request missing information, record its decision and limits, and keep the outcome current. Account holders and representatives complete their part through secure links without creating accounts, while the institution works from a shared role-based workspace. The result is a faster, easier-to-explain process with one auditable receipt—without Passage making the institution's legal decision or replacing its core systems.

## “For dummies” version

Passage is a shared checklist and receipt for a bank's power-of-attorney process. It gets the right information from each person, lets the bank decide, and makes sure everyone sees the same answer.

## Why this problem is worth solving

- The CFPB describes millions of Americans managing money or property for another person and publishes separate guidance for financial caregivers acting under a POA.
- CFPB guidance to financial institutions recommends processes that enable prompt POA decisions by qualified staff and help frontline employees identify possible abuse.
- The operating tension is real: institutions need enough certainty to protect customers while delayed or inconsistent handling can prevent an authorized representative from helping.
- The product opportunity is not another legal-document generator. It is a consistent operating layer between the people, the evidence, the institution decision, and the downstream record.

Sources: [CFPB financial caregiver guides](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/managing-someone-elses-money/), [CFPB recommendations for financial institutions](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201603_cfpb_recommendations-and-report-for-financial-institutions-on-preventing-and-responding-to-elder-financial-exploitation.pdf), and [ABA discussion of the institution perspective](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/publications/probate_property_magazine/v17/06/2003_aba_rpte_pp_v17_6_nov_dec_wentworth.authcheckdam.pdf).

## Initial ICP

Start with regional banks and credit unions that:

- service consumer deposit accounts in New York;
- receive enough POA requests for branch and back-office inconsistency to be visible;
- coordinate work through email, tickets, shared folders, spreadsheets, or disconnected checklists;
- have an identifiable deposit operations, member/customer operations, compliance operations, legal operations, or digital servicing owner;
- can sponsor one narrow workflow and involve an operations owner plus a security or technology reviewer.

Do not lead with the largest banks, institutions demanding an immediate core replacement, or buyers seeking automated legal opinions. The fastest design partner has a painful manual process, an executive sponsor, and permission to run a tightly bounded synthetic or controlled pilot.

## Ten discovery questions

1. Walk me through the last POA request from first contact to final account update.
2. Where can the request wait, get lost, or return for missing information?
3. Who is allowed to review it, and how does the frontline know it reached the right person?
4. How do you distinguish identity proof, document authority, and your institution's acceptance decision?
5. How do you record transaction limits, account scope, effective dates, and later revocation today?
6. What do the account holder and representative receive while the request is pending and after the decision?
7. How often do different teams give inconsistent instructions or request the same information twice?
8. Which systems need the final decision, and how is that update performed and checked?
9. What would a safe 60–90 day pilot have to prove for operations, compliance, security, and technology?
10. Who owns the budget and who could stop a pilot from proceeding?

## Qualification signals

- A named owner can describe the current workflow and quantify some combination of volume, elapsed time, handoffs, exceptions, complaints, or reviewer effort.
- The team agrees that “one current request and decision” is valuable even before deep core integration.
- A narrow geography, account type, team, and approved data boundary can be isolated for a pilot.
- Operations, compliance/legal, security/technology, procurement, and the economic buyer can be identified.

## Disqualification signals

- The buyer expects Passage to determine legal validity or guarantee acceptance.
- The project requires live money movement, automated transaction execution, or full core replacement in the first pilot.
- No owner can provide a controlled workflow, test users, or written success criteria.
- The institution requires unsupported certifications or production claims before discovery.

## Objection answers

**“We already handle this in our case-management system.”** Passage is valuable only if the existing process still leaves participants, reviewers, or downstream teams reconstructing scope and current status. The pilot measures that gap before proposing replacement or integration work.

**“Can your system approve a POA?”** No. Passage organizes evidence and the review process; the institution records and owns the final legal and operational decision.

**“Will customers need another account?”** No. Account holders and representatives use narrow, expiring links. Persistent accounts are for institution teams managing multiple requests.

**“Does this replace our core?”** No. The hosted-first pilot proves the workflow and produces a scoped result; integration then connects that result to the institution's system of record.

**“Is it enterprise ready?”** The controlled product has completed a hosted synthetic happy path. Independent persona replay, remaining negative paths, load and operating evidence, and the integration quickstart are explicit gates before a controlled institution pilot.

## Founding pilot offer

- $5,000 fixed fee for 60–90 days, credited toward year one if the institution converts under the pilot agreement.
- One institution, one New York financial-POA workflow, one named team, and one approved information boundary.
- Success measures agreed before configuration: completion rate, elapsed time, number of handoffs, missing-information loops, reviewer effort, decision consistency, receipt accuracy, and support burden.
- Sales-assisted agreement and invoice. No signup credit card, participant charge, or per-screen fee.
- The pilot ends with an evidence readout and a go/no-go decision on production scope, integration, controls, support, and annual commercial terms.

## Pricing hypotheses to test—not publish yet

1. Annual platform fee with an included request allowance best matches the institution's need for predictable procurement.
2. Volume bands work better than a visible microtransaction price because the value is operational control and risk reduction, not clicks.
3. Implementation, custom integration, and enhanced support should be scoped separately when they add real delivery cost.
4. A later product-led lane may offer one free activated request per verified organization each month, but only after automated verification, onboarding, abuse controls, and support recovery are proven.
5. Exact annual price, included volume, and overage remain open until at least ten qualified interviews and the first pilot reveal volume, cost to serve, willingness to pay, and procurement preference.

## Decisions to make after the next demo rehearsal

1. Choose the first 20 target institutions and the buyer role for each.
2. Set pilot success thresholds, not just metrics.
3. Choose the year-one price range to test in interviews without publishing it.
4. Decide whether the pilot uses synthetic data only or a separately approved controlled-data boundary.
5. Confirm the security, legal, integration, and support evidence each first design partner requires.

## Commercial sequence

1. Finish independent persona UAT and the repeatable seven-minute demo.
2. Close the negative-path, trust, operating, and integration-proof gates.
3. Run qualified discovery and demo conversations using only verified claims.
4. Refine the $5,000 pilot around observed urgency and written success thresholds.
5. Implement Stripe test invoices and HubSpot stages only when the journey and data fields are validated.
6. Enter a controlled design-partner pilot; use its evidence to set annual packaging.
