# Passage SOC 2 buyer answer

Updated September 7, 2026. Internal sales-preparation artifact; not permission to begin outreach or buyer demos.

## Thirty-second answer

“Passage does not currently have a SOC 2 report, and we do not describe an audit as underway. We have scoped the readiness work and keep evaluation synthetic. Before accepting real customer data, we will agree your assurance requirements, assign and fund the program, close the required controls, obtain auditor quotes, and select the appropriate Type I or Type II path with the CPA. We can walk your security team through the current architecture, shared-responsibility boundary, evidence already available, and the controls still open.”

## If the buyer asks for timing

“The immediate step is a bounded readiness phase: name the accountable owner, confirm scope, collect auditor quotes, and complete the highest-risk control evidence. A Type I decision follows readiness and buyer requirements. A Type II timeline depends on when the agreed controls are operating consistently and when the CPA begins the observation period. We will provide dates only after those dependencies are accepted.”

Do not offer a report date, say “SOC 2 in progress,” or imply an observation period has started without a named owner, approved funding, agreed scope, and documented auditor engagement.

## Evidence available for an early vendor review

- Synthetic-only evaluation boundary and explicit prohibition on real customer data.
- Application architecture and data-flow scope.
- Tenant, role, stale-session, recovery, receipt, and append-only event evidence.
- Current provider inventory and shared-responsibility boundary.
- Reconciliation, privileged MFA, backup/restore, incident, retention, privacy, vendor-risk, and independent security work tracked as open rather than overstated.
- A detailed readiness decision package with proposed control owners, budget reserve, auditor-selection rubric, and conditional stage gates.

## Internal decision sequence

1. Name the executive sponsor, program lead, control owners, and backups.
2. Approve a bounded readiness budget and evidence repository.
3. Confirm what the first qualified credit-union buyer requires for a synthetic evaluation and for a real-data pilot.
4. Collect comparable written auditor quotes and validate independence and financial-services SaaS experience.
5. Close and independently review privileged MFA, backups/restore, incident response, retention/deletion, vendor risk, privacy, secure development, and customer-assurance evidence.
6. Decide with the CPA whether a Type I report creates near-term commercial value.
7. Begin a Type II observation period only after the scoped controls operate consistently and the CPA agrees the start.

NCUA describes credit-union third-party review as risk-based and dependent on the institution and relationship. It does not establish a universal SOC 2 requirement for every pilot. Passage should nevertheless expect a security questionnaire and a request for current assurance evidence or a credible plan.
