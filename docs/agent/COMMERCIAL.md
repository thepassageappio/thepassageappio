# Commercial playbook

Load for positioning, pricing, buyer deck, outreach, CRM strategy, or LinkedIn.

## Position

- Category: the institution's operating record for delegated authority after the document arrives.
- Lead: **One institution decision, with clear scope and a record of what changes later.**
- Passage coordinates intake, evidence, review, scoped decision, matching receipt, and lifecycle. The institution retains legal, identity, fraud, policy, and access-control responsibility.

## Pricing

- Founding pilot: `$5,000`, 60–90 days, one institution, one team, one workflow, credited toward year one.
- Annual discovery anchors: `$12k`, `$24k`, `$36k` for the same bounded scope. Do not publish as tiers before buyer evidence.
- Do not claim ROI, savings, fraud reduction, compliance, certification, or enterprise readiness without evidence.

## Distribution gate

P0 demo readiness is closed. P1 infrastructure is prepared but not launched, and P2 is mid-flight. One-pager, buyer deck, cold sequence, LinkedIn article/posts, and buyer-facing demos remain drafted and held until **P1 and P2 both close**. Internal target research and personalization may continue.

## Buyer-ready SOC 2 answer

Prepare this before the first sales conversation rather than waiting for the end of P2. NCUA guidance makes third-party due diligence proportional to the credit union's risk and the relationship; it does not create a universal SOC 2-report requirement for every pilot. Passage should still expect security/vendor-risk questions and arrive with a concrete path.

Approved current answer: “Passage does not currently have a SOC 2 report or an audit underway. We have a scoped readiness package and a synthetic-data evaluation boundary. Before accepting real customer data, we will agree the buyer's assurance requirements, assign and fund the program, close the required controls, obtain auditor quotes, and select the appropriate Type I/Type II path with the CPA.”

Near-term decision sequence: name sponsor and program lead; approve a readiness budget; collect comparable auditor quotes; validate buyer requirements; finish privileged MFA, backups/restore, incident, retention, vendor-risk, privacy, and security evidence; then decide whether a Type I is commercially useful and when a Type II observation period can honestly begin. Keep dates conditional until an auditor and owners accept them.
