# Plain language on every Passage screen

Owner direction, September 10, 2026: all website and product language must be easy to understand, natural, and useful. Aim for the simplicity of explaining a step to a five-year-old while treating the adult reader with respect. Do not claim a measured reading age without user testing.

## Write for the person doing the task

- Say what happened, who needs to act, and what to do next.
- Use short sentences and familiar words. Give one instruction at a time.
- Name the action on buttons: Save draft, Send request, Open receipt.
- Explain necessary terms where people first need them. An institution is the bank or credit union. An account holder owns the account. A representative is the person asking to help. A receipt is the saved decision.
- Use actual names when that makes the next step clearer.
- Errors explain the problem and a practical next step. Never promise that nothing changed unless that is known.
- Avoid internal terms in ordinary guidance: activation, lifecycle, entitlement, scope boundary, role-bound, controlled inbox, downstream, operational fit, and go-or-no-go readout.
- Avoid empty sales claims, slogans, forced contrasts, and sentences that sound like a list of abstract nouns. Read the sentence aloud. If it sounds unnatural, rewrite it.
- Keep requested actions separate from accepted actions. Keep sending an email separate from confirmed delivery. Keep a saved decision separate from account access.
- Preserve exact accepted terms, consent, policy snapshots, and saved history. Give them a plain explanation. Changes to binding text require a new version through the existing process.
- Technical documentation may use precise technical terms, with an explanation. Never rename API fields, event codes, or legal concepts just to improve a reading score.

## Review coverage

Apply this to public pages, metadata, navigation, buttons, forms, hints, empty states, errors, status messages, participant steps, receipts, team/security/billing guidance, emails, articles and guides. Include phone layouts and screen-reader labels. A wording change is not finished until it fits its screen and still means the same thing.

This is a permanent product acceptance rule, not a one-time copy cleanup.
