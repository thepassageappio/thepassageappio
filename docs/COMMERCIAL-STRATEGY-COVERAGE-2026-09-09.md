## Customer journey: current map and open work

The existing `CUSTOMER-JOURNEY-AND-GTM.md` covers Discover, Evaluate, Experience, Validate, Pilot, and Convert. The product itself separately proves owner, principal, representative, and reviewer steps. The missing commercial lifecycle stages are adoption after contract, ongoing engagement, renewal/expansion, advocacy/referral, and churn prevention.

Current emotional hypothesis: concern and ambiguity trigger discovery; skepticism dominates evaluation; clarity rises when each persona sees one next action; confidence peaks at the matching bounded receipt; procurement/security/legal review creates a second dip; successful configuration and a first real institution-approved outcome should create the retention peak. This remains a hypothesis until buyer and participant interviews supply direct language and measured drop-off.

Completion artifact: one lifecycle map with customer action, thought/emotion, digital and physical touchpoints, friction, delight opportunity, metric, owner, and intervention for every stage from awareness through churn.

## Buyer segmentation: current hypothesis and research need

The primary segment remains community and regional banks and credit unions with visible manual POA handling across branches, inboxes, tickets, shared folders, or inconsistent checklists. Likely champion functions are deposit/member operations, compliance operations, legal operations, and digital servicing. Security, technology, procurement, legal, the operational owner, and the economic buyer form the buying committee.

The four useful research personas should be job-based rather than demographic stereotypes:

1. Operations owner responsible for throughput, handoffs, staffing, and complaints.
2. Compliance/legal reviewer responsible for policy consistency, exceptions, defensibility, and retention.
3. Technology/security evaluator responsible for data boundaries, identity, integration, resilience, and vendor risk.
4. Executive/economic sponsor responsible for priority, budget, customer/member experience, and pilot outcomes.

Participant personas remain essential for product UX but are not the same as paying-customer segmentation. Complete each buyer persona only after direct interviews establish pains, triggers, objections, information sources, buying behavior, success measures, and willingness-to-pay signals.

## Trends and strategic structure

The trend brief and Five Forces analysis require fresh primary-source research because regulations, competitors, investment, platform capabilities, and buyer expectations can change. Research should cover delegated authority and vulnerable-customer operations, aging and caregiving, branch-to-digital servicing, identity and fraud controls, institution third-party risk, state POA modernization, estate/death notification, signed decision APIs, and investment or consolidation around identity, legal-tech, fintech infrastructure, and servicing workflow.

Do not promote the multi-recipient authority/life-event network or participant portfolio as current functionality. Treat them as promising V3 hypotheses: one underlying authority relationship or life event can create several confidential recipient-specific cases, while every institution or service provider independently applies its policy and records its own result.
